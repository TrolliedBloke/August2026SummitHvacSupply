# Summit HVAC Supply production-readiness audit

**Audit date:** August 4, 2026
**Scope:** Entire Next.js storefront and operational surface, desktop at 1440 x 900, mobile at 390 x 844, source review, data and checkout paths, metadata, automated tests, and category benchmarks.
**Method:** Four independent lenses: retail customer, wholesale buyer, senior product designer, and creative director. This is a launch audit, not a portfolio critique.

**Impact scale used throughout:** Critical = High expected impact and launch-blocking risk; Major = High or Medium expected impact; Minor = Low expected impact in isolation. Each finding states the practical outcome expected from its fix.

## Executive verdict

**Do not launch this as a transactional ecommerce site yet.** The interface is visually credible and the main journeys are understandable, but the operational truth beneath them is not production-ready. Public stock, prices, product documents, and much of the catalog come from demo data. The configured Supabase checkout path uses the public key against policies that permit only staff inserts, so a real guest checkout is expected to fail. Those are not polish issues; they break the core promise of “parts on the shelf.”

The strongest part is the interaction concept: exact-model search, visible Newark pickup, part-number-first product identity, gated trade pricing, and a restrained supply-counter visual system. The weakest part is commercial integrity: catalog depth, inventory authority, document authenticity, checkout reliability, delivery promises, and compatibility data.

**Production readiness: 4.3/10.**
**Visual concept readiness: 7.5/10.**
**Commerce operations readiness: 2.4/10.**

## Launch blockers

### 1. Public inventory, prices, and catalog are demo data — Critical

- **What is wrong:** `src/lib/storefront/catalog.ts:3-8` imports `demoInventoryLots`, `demoSkuDocuments`, `demoSkus`, and `demoWarehouses`; `getStorefrontSkus()` at line 61 always maps that data. The live storefront never queries an inventory, pricing, or product source of record.
- **Why users notice:** Stock counts, prices, delivery decisions, and product assortment can disagree with the counter. A contractor who drives to Newark for a nonexistent unit will not trust the site again.
- **Why engineers/designers notice:** Every high-confidence UI treatment is fed by fixture data. The visual hierarchy overstates certainty that the system cannot support.
- **Business and conversion impact:** Overselling, incorrect pricing, chargebacks, counter conflict, paid-media feed rejection, and permanent trust loss. This blocks ecommerce launch.
- **Implementation:** Introduce a server-side catalog repository backed by the production PIM/ERP or Supabase tables; read branch-level available-to-promise inventory, customer-tier prices, and product status. Add cache tags, stale-data timestamps, reconciliation jobs, and fail-closed behavior when freshness expires. Keep demo data behind an explicit non-production flag.
- **Expected improvement:** Real purchase confidence, operationally safe pickup, usable Shopping feeds, and a foundation for scale.
- **Effort:** Large.

### 2. Configured production checkout uses the wrong Supabase client — Critical

- **What is wrong:** `src/lib/backend/checkout.ts:67` calls `createServerSupabaseClient()`. That function uses the public publishable/anon key in `src/lib/backend/supabase.ts:22-31`, despite the checkout comment calling it service-role. RLS only gives staff full access to `sales_orders` (`supabase/migrations/001_wholesale_demo.sql:444-445`), and `reserve_public_order` is granted only to `service_role` (`006_fulfillment.sql:139`). Guest and normal trade inserts are therefore expected to fail once Supabase is configured.
- **Why users notice:** The final order action fails after the user has spent time completing checkout.
- **Why engineers/designers notice:** The seeded no-database fallback makes the demo look healthy while masking the production authorization failure.
- **Business and conversion impact:** Near-total checkout loss and support burden. This blocks launch.
- **Implementation:** Prefer one narrowly scoped `security definer` checkout RPC that validates SKU, quantity, price tier, inventory, account, tax, and fulfillment and atomically creates/reserves the order. Alternatively use the service-role client only inside a server-only validated action. Add idempotency keys, transaction rollback, migrated-Supabase integration tests, and alerts on failed checkout stages.
- **Expected improvement:** Reliable order creation with no partial orders or orphaned reservations.
- **Effort:** Medium to large.

### 3. Product documents are synthetic placeholders — Critical

- **What is wrong:** `src/app/api/documents/[id]/route.ts:9-30` creates a one-page PDF containing “verified launch document” and a mock storage path. It is presented as a submittal/manual.
- **Why users notice:** Contractors open documents to confirm wiring, dimensions, clearances, AHRI combinations, and installation requirements. A placeholder immediately disqualifies the supplier.
- **Why engineers/designers notice:** The interface labels the artifact with authority it does not possess.
- **Business and conversion impact:** Abandoned projects, incorrect installs, liability, quote loss, and reputational damage. This blocks launch.
- **Implementation:** Store original manufacturer PDFs in versioned object storage; record manufacturer, document type, revision, locale, publication date, checksum, and affected SKUs. Serve ungated files with immutable caching and a clear revision label. Do not show a document type until the real file exists.
- **Expected improvement:** Professional confidence and lower pre-sale support load.
- **Effort:** Medium.

### 4. Product specifications and system matches are not governed — Critical

- **What is wrong:** Product records contain fields still marked for confirmation, while PDPs present specs and an “AHRI-certified” system-builder treatment as settled facts. The system builder uses generic matching and repeated series imagery rather than an authoritative compatibility record.
- **Why users notice:** HVAC buyers depend on exact voltage, refrigerant, capacity, pairing, dimensions, efficiency, and certificate numbers. One wrong field can invalidate a permit, rebate, or warranty.
- **Business and conversion impact:** Wrong equipment combinations, returns, field delays, and liability.
- **Implementation:** Create a verified product-information workflow with source URL/file, reviewer, reviewed date, revision, confidence state, and publication gate. Model compatible systems as explicit manufacturer/AHRI records, not category proximity. Hide unverified claims.
- **Expected improvement:** Fewer returns and support calls; credible technical purchasing.
- **Effort:** Large.

### 5. Payment and inventory reservation are not one reliable state machine — Critical

- **What is wrong:** `placeOrder()` creates and reserves the order before Stripe confirmation. If Stripe is absent, the order can still return without a client secret. No visible reservation expiry, payment timeout recovery, or end-to-end idempotency contract protects inventory.
- **Why users notice:** A buyer can believe an order exists while payment is incomplete; inventory can remain unavailable to everyone else.
- **Business and conversion impact:** Unpaid reservations, duplicate attempts, inventory distortion, and reconciliation work.
- **Implementation:** Define `draft -> payment_pending -> paid -> allocated -> fulfilled` and terminal failure/expiry states. Use a checkout idempotency key, short reservation TTL, Stripe webhook as payment authority, retry-safe handlers, and a recovery page for interrupted payment.
- **Expected improvement:** Accurate inventory and fewer payment disputes.
- **Effort:** Large.

### Retail findings

#### Category promise exceeds actual assortment — Major

- **Wrong:** Homepage tiles advertise furnaces, water heating, refrigerant, parts, sheet metal, tools, thermostats, and clearance, but most route to a generic destination and the catalog contains only 16 TCL equipment SKUs.
- **Why it matters:** Recognition without fulfillment is worse than a narrower honest taxonomy. Home Depot and SupplyHouse can support breadth; Summit currently cannot.
- **Solution:** Publish only categories with useful inventory and landing pages. Mark coming categories internally, not publicly. Build category-specific filters, education, and stock totals before exposing each one.
- **Expected impact:** Lower pogo-sticking and more credible assortment perception. **Effort: Small now; large for true expansion.**

#### “14 on the shelf” misstates the metric — Major

- **Wrong:** The homepage count is the number of SKUs with positive stock, not the number of physical units. “On the shelf” reads as unit quantity.
- **Solution:** Use “14 stocked models” or show true sum of available units and a last-updated timestamp. Never blend SKU count and unit count.
- **Expected impact:** More honest pickup confidence. **Effort: Small.**

#### PDP action is below the mobile fold — Major

- **Wrong:** At 390 x 844 the product image consumes most of the first viewport; availability begins near the bottom and purchase buttons appear around 1,050 px. The existing `StickyBuyBar` is not mounted on SKU pages.
- **Why it matters:** High-intent users must scroll before they can reserve the item they searched for. Fitts’s Law favors a persistent, reachable primary action.
- **Solution:** Reduce mobile gallery height, surface price and Newark stock immediately, and add a bottom sticky bar with quantity and “Reserve for pickup.” Respect safe-area insets and avoid covering content.
- **Expected impact:** Higher mobile add-to-cart and pickup starts. **Effort: Medium.**

#### Media does not answer pre-purchase questions — Major

- **Wrong:** Each SKU gets one repeated series image and a generated rectangle labeled as dimensions. There are no real labels, connection views, outdoor/indoor pairing views, nameplates, scale references, or manufacturer drawings.
- **Solution:** Require a media checklist per equipment type: front, side, connections, nameplate, included parts, dimensional drawing, and installed-context photo where useful. Maintain accurate alt text and zoom.
- **Expected impact:** Fewer compatibility questions and returns. **Effort: Large.**

#### Delivery and branch claims are not authoritative — Major

- **Wrong:** PDP cards list Hayward, San Jose, and Sacramento as “Call to confirm” while Summit’s actual fulfillment hub is Newark. Out-of-stock receipts are hardcoded to 7–10 business days; client delivery estimates use a generic business-day rule.
- **Solution:** Show only real locations and live transfers. Compute pickup cutoff, local route date, freight estimate, and incoming PO ETA from operating data. Phrase estimates as estimates until carrier/route confirmation.
- **Expected impact:** Less disappointment and fewer “where is my order?” contacts. **Effort: Large.**

#### Homeowner guidance still stops short of safe selection — Major

- **Wrong:** The site helps route buyers but does not provide a verified compatibility path for equipment, electrical, line-set, controls, permits, and installer scope.
- **Solution:** Ask structured questions, explain what the result can and cannot determine, require installer confirmation for refrigerant/240V equipment, and return compatible bundles only from verified records.
- **Expected impact:** More qualified homeowner leads with fewer wrong-cart events. **Effort: Large.**

#### Social proof is absent in production — Major

- **Wrong:** Demo testimonials/reviews are intentionally hidden in production and there is no verified customer evidence to replace them.
- **Solution:** Launch a consented review program tied to fulfilled orders; distinguish product reviews from Summit service reviews. Show reviewer type, city, purchase context, date, and verified-purchase status. Never present invented testimonials.
- **Expected impact:** Higher trust for first-time retail buyers. **Effort: Medium.**

#### Checkout is long and loses action context — Major

- **Wrong:** Mobile checkout presents many pickup-window controls and contact fields before the order summary and final action. ZIP and quantity controls are smaller than the rest.
- **Solution:** Use progressive disclosure by fulfillment method, keep a collapsible sticky order summary/action, use 44 px minimum controls, and show validation beside the field. Preserve cart and form state across errors.
- **Expected impact:** Lower mobile checkout abandonment. **Effort: Medium.**

### Wholesale findings

#### No true contractor quick order — Major

- **Wrong:** A contractor can search and add products individually, but cannot paste a list of model numbers and quantities, upload CSV, enter customer PO metadata in advance, or resolve exceptions in one table.
- **Solution:** Add a Quick Order surface with paste/CSV, per-line match confidence, stock, tier price, substitutes, validation, and one-click cart creation. Preserve unmatched rows for correction.
- **Expected impact:** Materially faster recurring orders and higher contractor retention. **Effort: Large.**

#### Account approval lacks transparency — Major

- **Wrong:** The flow moves to phone/email follow-up and promises review timing without showing required documents, ownership, status, or missing information.
- **Solution:** Support license/resale certificate upload, explicit buyer types, application checklist, status history, SLA, secure messaging, and a clear path for property managers who are not contractors.
- **Expected impact:** Fewer abandoned applications and staff follow-ups. **Effort: Medium.**

#### Pricing model is too simple for B2B — Major

- **Wrong:** The code switches between MSRP and one dealer price. Real wholesale needs account agreements, quantity breaks, contract pricing, tax status, quote validity, freight, and branch terms.
- **Solution:** Add a pricing service with account/ship-to/quantity/date context and explain whether a price is list, contract, promotional, or quoted. Keep public and trade prices clearly separated.
- **Expected impact:** Fewer pricing disputes and better account conversion. **Effort: Large.**

#### Procurement workflows are incomplete — Major

- **Wrong:** No multi-user company roles, approval thresholds, cost centers, ship-to management, buyer permissions, quote-to-order audit trail, or downloadable order exports are evident.
- **Solution:** Add organization accounts with admin/buyer/approver roles, PO rules, saved ship-tos, approval routing, quote versions, invoice/document exports, and reorder templates.
- **Expected impact:** Makes Summit viable for property portfolios, builders, and procurement teams. **Effort: Large.**

#### Search needs professional controls at scale — Major

- **Wrong:** Current fuzzy search is excellent for 16 SKUs but lacks manufacturer facets, compatible replacement chains, previous-purchase ranking, exact-match pinning rules, and a visible no-result recovery process at catalog scale.
- **Solution:** Preserve the existing normalization, add synonym/cross-reference governance, account-aware ranking, stock and branch facets, recent searches, and zero-result merchandising. Measure search-to-PDP and zero-result rates.
- **Expected impact:** Contractors can find exact parts in under 30 seconds as inventory grows. **Effort: Large.**

### Product-design findings

#### Recognition and system status are visually strong but factually weak — Major

- **Principle:** Nielsen’s visibility-of-system-status heuristic requires truthful, current feedback, not merely prominent feedback.
- **Wrong:** Green stock, branch cards, cutoff copy, and receipt estimates look authoritative without live authority.
- **Solution:** Add source timestamp, branch identity, reserved quantity logic, and explicit stale/unavailable states. Fail closed rather than inventing continuity.
- **Expected impact:** Trustworthy status cues and fewer operational errors. **Effort: Large.**

#### Too many small mobile targets — Major

- **Wrong:** Audited header controls are 40 x 40 and many footer/example links expose only 17–20 px of height; ZIP and quantity controls are also undersized.
- **Solution:** Enforce a 44 x 44 minimum interactive area token, including invisible padding where visual size must remain compact. Keep at least 8 px between adjacent targets.
- **Expected impact:** WCAG 2.2 target-size improvement and fewer touch errors. **Effort: Small to medium.**

#### Muted text fails normal-text contrast — Major

- **Wrong:** `#8A8880` on `#FAFAF8` measures approximately 3.40:1 and on white 3.55:1. Normal text requires 4.5:1. There are roughly 142 `text-ink-3`/`text-ink-4` uses.
- **Solution:** Darken the muted token to pass 4.5:1 on both surfaces, then reserve lighter values for non-text decoration or large text only. Run automated contrast checks in CI.
- **Expected impact:** WCAG AA legibility, particularly for metadata and help text. **Effort: Small.**

#### Product zoom dialog lacks complete modal behavior — Major

- **Wrong:** `product-gallery.tsx` supports Escape but does not trap focus, move focus into the dialog, restore focus to the opener, or mark the background inert.
- **Solution:** Use the project’s accessible dialog primitive or implement focus capture/restore, tab containment, background inertness, labelled title, and scroll lock.
- **Expected impact:** Keyboard and screen-reader completion. **Effort: Medium.**

#### Chat competes with primary tasks — Minor

- **Wrong:** The fixed chat launcher overlaps cards/content on mobile and remains visually prominent during checkout and PDP evaluation.
- **Solution:** Offset it above sticky actions, collapse it on checkout, respect safe areas, and test every viewport. Trigger proactive chat only from observed hesitation, never immediately.
- **Expected impact:** Less obstruction and distraction. **Effort: Small.**

#### Design-system rules are not fully enforced — Minor

- **Wrong:** Theme guidance limits weights to 400/500 and green to availability/action, yet `font-semibold` and decorative green icon treatments remain. Sentence-case rules also drift.
- **Solution:** Encode weight, color-role, radius, and casing rules in primitives and lintable utilities. Remove one-off semantic color use.
- **Expected impact:** More coherent, maintainable brand expression. **Effort: Medium.**

#### Homepage initial image policy is wasteful — Major

- **Wrong:** The logo, two door illustrations, and all 12 category illustrations are preload/eager. Below-the-fold line art competes with critical font and hero resources.
- **Solution:** Preload only the LCP candidate and logo if justified; lazy-load below-fold categories; set explicit dimensions; measure LCP, INP, CLS, and resource priority in production.
- **Expected impact:** Faster mobile LCP and less bandwidth. **Effort: Small.**

## SEO and AI-discovery audit

- **Major — inherited canonical:** Root metadata sets `canonical: "/"` in `src/app/layout.tsx:44-46`. Only local and SKU pages override it, so homeowners, products, about, contact, dealer, and many resource pages can canonicalize to the homepage. Add a self-referencing canonical per indexable route or remove the root canonical and generate route-aware metadata.
- **Major — structured data depends on demo truth:** Product offer price and availability schema mirror mock catalog data. Search engines can treat mismatched structured data as misleading. Publish schema only from the same authoritative source visible to users.
- **Major — category intent is thin:** Category pages need unique copy, real inventory, faceted links, breadcrumbs, compatibility explanations, and FAQs. Do not index empty or generic category promises.
- **Major — location coverage mismatch:** Bay Area language is broad while local-delivery eligibility is narrow. Separate service-area, pickup, local-delivery, installer-referral, and freight claims by ZIP.
- **Minor — AI citation readiness:** `llms.txt`, Organization schema, FAQ schema, and descriptive pages are useful, but retrieval systems need reliable factual pages: branch details, current inventory semantics, sourced rebate updates, policies, product revisions, and author/reviewer/date fields.
- **Best practice:** Maintain XML sitemaps by type and last modification, Product/Breadcrumb/LocalBusiness schema, image alt text, 301 handling for retired SKUs, replacement-product links, and Search Console/Bing monitoring.

## Benchmark assessment

- [Baymard’s ecommerce search research](https://baymard.com/research/ecommerce-search) reinforces that product findability determines whether users can buy. Summit’s forgiving part-number search is pointed in the right direction, but it needs production-scale indexing, no-result recovery, and behavioral measurement.
- [Baymard’s payment UX guidance](https://baymard.com/learn/payment-ux) emphasizes confidence at payment and review. Summit must make payment state, inventory reservation, total, and recovery unambiguous.
- [SupplyHouse multi-zone listings](https://www.supplyhouse.com/Multi-Zone-Mini-Split-Air-Conditioners-36110000) combine model identity, price, availability, compare, and purchase controls at catalog scale. Summit is cleaner but far shallower and less operationally grounded.
- [SupplyHouse mini-split navigation](https://www.supplyhouse.com/Mini-Split-Air-Conditioners-1811000/) connects systems, selection education, and accessories. Summit needs the same rigor without exposing unverified combinations.
- [Ferguson’s residential HVAC taxonomy](https://www.ferguson.com/category/heating-cooling/residential-equipment/) demonstrates deep part/manufacturer identifiers and account-aware availability. Summit’s simplified model works only while the catalog is tiny.
- [Grainger’s distribution model](https://www.grainger.com/content/retail-distribution) makes branch capability and availability a core operating promise. Summit visually imitates this confidence before it has the data and network behind it.
- [McMaster-Carr’s ordering model](https://www.mcmaster.com/international/) minimizes account friction while preserving history and documents. Summit should let retail buyers finish quickly and let business accounts progressively add controls.
- [Nielsen Norman Group’s usability heuristics](https://media.nngroup.com/media/articles/attachments/Heuristic_Summary1_A4_compressed.pdf) support the core recommendations: truthful system status, real-world language, consistency, error prevention, recognition, and recovery.

Summit does **not** yet meet Grainger, McMaster-Carr, SupplyHouse, Ferguson, Home Depot, Amazon, or Amazon Business on assortment, operational data, fulfillment, procurement, or reliability. Visually, it is more disciplined than many regional distributors but does not reach Apple, Stripe, Shopify, Vercel, Linear, or Figma standards because the asset system, interaction states, accessibility details, and content truth are incomplete.

## Marketing and growth strategy

### Positioning

Own a narrow, believable promise first: **“Search live Newark HVAC stock. Reserve it for pickup. Get a real person when the model match matters.”** Homeowner support is a secondary path: equipment guidance plus qualified installer referral, never implied installation by Summit.

### Homepage and PDP copy improvements

### Lifecycle, merchandising, and retention

- **Homepage merchandising:** Use live operational modules, not generic promotions: recently received models, confirmed pickup-ready systems, A2L-compliant replacements, and a homeowner decision lane. Limit the page to inventory Summit can actually fulfill.
- **Landing pages:** Create one page per meaningful intent: exact model/series, Newark pickup, Bay Area homeowner system help, contractor account, multi-unit property quote, and verified rebate program. Each page needs one dominant outcome and an audience-specific proof set.
- **Email:** Build consented flows for application status, saved-cart reminder, quote follow-up, pickup-ready, order status, post-purchase document bundle, warranty registration reminder, replenishment, and review request. Stop a flow immediately when its objective is completed.
- **Seasonal campaigns:** Trigger campaigns from local weather and operations: first heat wave, smoke/IAQ season, preseason contractor stocking, first cold snap, and verified incentive deadlines. Avoid vague percentage-off events on constrained equipment.
- **Customer retention:** Save model history, purchases, documents, job lists, preferred branch/fulfillment, and compatible reorder items. Make the portal more useful after purchase than the inbox.
- **Referral program:** Separate contractor referrals from homeowner referrals. Track consent, lead acceptance, service area, response time, and closed-job quality. Reward only compliant completed outcomes, not raw names.
- **Product merchandising:** Rank by availability and fit, not margin alone. Show exact included components, required accessories, compatible alternatives, and why one option costs more. Never cross-sell unverified equipment pairings.
- **Promotional banners:** One message, one condition, one destination. Preferred example: “Order confirmed in-stock items by 2 pm for same-day Newark pickup.” Show only while the cutoff and inventory system are reliable.

## Final approval decision

**Would I approve this website for production? No, not for transactions.** I would approve a clearly labelled private demo or a public lead-generation/catalog preview only if price, stock, documents, checkout, reviews, financing, branch, and category claims that are not operationally true are removed or marked unavailable.

**Would I approve it as the primary ecommerce site for a company targeting $100M+ annual revenue? No.** The design direction can support that ambition, but the current platform cannot. Before launch, the first five critical blockers must be fixed and tested with real systems; the assortment and branch claims must become honest; WCAG AA failures must be resolved; and real product media, documents, compatibility, reviews, fulfillment, and account workflows must replace placeholders.

The site is not far from looking launch-ready. It is far from being transaction-ready. That distinction is the central finding of this audit.
