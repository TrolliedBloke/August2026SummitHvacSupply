# Advertising disclosure — draft for counsel

Status: proposed language only, not published or approved. Prepared October 1, 2026. The live notice remains version 1.1, `PRIVACY_DISCLOSES_AD_SHARING` remains false, and advertising gates remain closed.

## Proposed replacement for the no-sharing section

### Advertising and your privacy choices

If you allow advertising cookies, we use Meta and Google advertising tools to measure our campaigns and show relevant Summit ads. These tools may receive identifiers such as cookie identifiers, IP addresses, browser information, pages visited, and interactions with our website. This disclosure can constitute sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising under California law.

Where the applicable notice and your choices permit it, we may also provide Meta and Google with hashed email addresses for customer matching. This helps us reach broad groups, such as contractors or homeowners planning a project, and avoid showing acquisition ads to existing customers. Hashing changes an email into a coded value; it does not make the information anonymous. We do not include your project notes or detailed finder answers in audience uploads.

You can opt out using [Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information](/privacy/opt-out), without creating an account. We honor Global Privacy Control signals. An opt-out applies to the browser sending it and, when we can associate it with your email, to that email in future audience exports. You can also provide your email on the opt-out page to record that preference. Staff handle removal from audiences already uploaded to advertising platforms.

Your advertising choices do not affect your ability to browse, request a quote, or buy from Summit. Email marketing has a separate opt-in and an unsubscribe link. Asking us to email your finder results does not by itself subscribe you to marketing or authorize advertising sharing.

## Questions and publication prerequisites

- Determine whether Summit meets a CCPA applicability threshold using actual revenue and qualifying processing volumes. Website visits alone do not establish a threshold. Confirm current inflation-adjusted thresholds and affiliated-business treatment.
- Review whether the selected Meta/Google uses constitute sale as well as sharing, and adjust the heading and disclosure accordingly. Do not retain an unqualified “we do not sell” statement without reviewing the arrangements.
- Confirm the categories of identifiers, internet activity, purchase information, and inferences actually disclosed, recipients, purposes, retention, and required prior-12-month disclosures. Final text must match enabled tags and uploads.
- Approve the collection notice and the permission required for Customer Match and Meta audiences. Existing email consent under the no-sharing notice is not retroactive permission for advertising. The export implementation requires consent under the current notice version, but the collection checkbox itself must be reviewed before activation.
- Decide how to handle GPC for recognized people across devices, requests concerning previously uploaded audiences, retention/deletion, authorized agents, and minors. Assign a staff owner and platform-removal process before the first upload.
- Confirm Google Customer Match account eligibility and Meta terms in the actual advertising accounts. An email count is an internal readiness gate, not a guarantee of platform eligibility or match rates.
- Publish a newly versioned, approved notice with its previous version archived. Only then consider enabling advertising configuration. Check browser opt-outs, consent, CSP, platform removal procedures, and the audience export audit trail before launch.

## Primary references checked

- [CPPA frequently asked questions](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq): opt-out preference signals, privacy policy disclosures, and consumer rights.
- [California Attorney General CCPA guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa): applicability and privacy rights; counsel should confirm current thresholds.
- [Google Customer Match policy](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6299717?hl=en): first-party data, disclosure, and consent requirements.

The proposed wording describes intended future operations, not a representation that ads are already running. Counsel and operations must reconcile it with the selected vendors and practices before publication.
